### Families

The ladders below distinguish increasing responsibility within each family; they are analytical comparisons, not standardized maturity scales.

**1. Adaptive case management — high confidence in the structural mapping.**

The unit of work is an evolving case: conversation, evidence, prior agreements, pending questions and responses. Changes make activities relevant; investigation can discover additional activities; a human retains authority over consequential decisions. AI and people can perform activities within the same case. OMG's case-management account explicitly covers evolving information, unpredictable activity order and human discretion. Its CMMN specification includes information, timer and user events that enable or terminate activities. [OMG overview](https://www.omg.org/cmmn/), [CMMN specification](https://www.omg.org/spec/CMMN/1.1/PDF).

Its ladder runs from **recording cases → enabling and tracking case activities → assisting investigation → performing bounded case work**. The supplied shape reaches the last position while leaving shared decision-making with a human. That placement is an inference, with high confidence.

Structural failure mechanisms, inferred with medium-to-high confidence:

- Treating an evolving investigation as a fixed sequence can produce nominal completion without answering the case.
- A changed case file can invalidate an investigation already underway.
- Manual and automated workers can each complete the same activity without recognizing the other's contribution.
- A recorded response can be mistaken for resolution although the case still contains an unanswered obligation.

Case management explains the whole work arrangement. It does not, by itself, establish interruption recovery or public-write correctness.

**2. Durable, reactive workflow orchestration — high confidence in the structural mapping.**

A changed conversation starts or advances a workflow; investigation and publication are external activities; human decisions arrive as messages; persisted progress permits recovery after interruption. Manual work changes the surrounding world or supplies messages to the running execution. Temporal provides a concrete primary-source example of durable workflows receiving messages and waiting for human input. [Workflow messages](https://docs.temporal.io/encyclopedia/workflow-message-passing), [Durable human interaction](https://learn.temporal.io/tutorials/ai/building-durable-ai-applications/human-in-the-loop/).

Its ladder runs from **scheduled execution → retryable jobs → persisted multistep execution → long-lived execution with external participants**. The description places the shape at the last position as a requirement, with high confidence; it supplies no evidence that this capability exists.

One established failure mechanism is particularly relevant: an external action can succeed, then the worker can fail before recording success. Recovery may repeat that action. Workflow durability does not automatically make external effects occur once. Temporal documents this distinction explicitly. Applied here, publication could repeat even though workflow recovery works correctly. Confidence: high. [Activity execution and idempotency](https://docs.temporal.io/activity-definition).

Other structural applications are inferences, with medium-to-high confidence: resumed work can use evidence that has changed; a human's manual completion can make a pending automated action obsolete; preserving an approval does not establish that its scope still covers a changed result.

**3. Policy-bounded supervisory control — medium confidence in the whole-shape mapping.**

Observation maps to monitoring; investigation and agreement evaluation to analysis; choosing a response or escalation to planning; public response to execution. Accepted agreements supply policy and knowledge. Humans govern policy changes, while independently operated teams remain outside the worker's direct control. IBM's MAPE account supplies this structural decomposition, including policy-guided planning. [IBM autonomic-computing architecture](https://www.redbooks.ibm.com/redbooks/pdfs/sg246665.pdf).

Its ladder runs from **observation → advice → execution within supplied policy → changing policy autonomously**. The description reaches bounded execution and explicitly excludes autonomous policy change. Confidence: high about the supplied authority boundary; medium about calling the complete arrangement a control system.

The qualification matters: public responses constitute feedback only if they influence subsequent work and that influence is observable. Otherwise, the program is repeatedly producing assessments rather than regulating a system. Kubernetes's controller account illustrates the stronger condition: observations and actions are related to an identifiable desired state. [Controller model](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/architecture/controller/).

Structural failure mechanisms, inferred with medium confidence:

- The worker's own responses can become fresh triggers, creating a feedback cycle.
- Delayed evidence can provoke responses to conditions already corrected manually.
- Applying ambiguous agreements can quietly introduce policy while appearing to execute policy.
- Independent actors can pursue different objectives, so repeated intervention need not converge.
- A “healthy” tracker state can diverge from the actual condition of the participating projects.
