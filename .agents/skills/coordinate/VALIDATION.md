# Coordination validation

The coordinator owns these criteria. Apply them during production and validation,
relative to the declared scope and stage. A clean structural check does not prove
semantic compatibility.

## Clarity

The next reader can determine the current outcome, scope, responsible parties,
governing authority, required action, dependencies, permitted internal choices
and acceptance evidence. References are accessible in that reader's environment.
No Workshop conversation or repository-layout knowledge is assumed of recipients.
Current briefs contain no obsolete instructions or irrelevant investigation history.

## Consistency

Observations retain their provenance; accepted promises are not rewritten to fit
code. Shared obligations retain one meaning through reconciliation, decomposition,
recipient instructions and acceptance. Read the full
[contract-shape criteria](CONTRACT-SHAPE.md), including common conformance cases.
Every applicable dimension is settled for implementation work; inapplicable
dimensions carry a reason. No source adds unapproved guarantees.

Provider and consumer assignments are specialized projections of the same authority,
not independent contract definitions. Their actions and evidence may differ.
Review viable transition states as well as the endpoint. No pair of implementations
satisfying their respective briefs may disagree on a shared shape or guarantee.

## Completeness

Every in-scope obligation has a responsible action and acceptance proof, or
evidence it is already fulfilled. Cover work between projects and the proof of
the joint outcome; two local successes alone need not establish integration.
Record exclusions, deferrals and unknowns explicitly under their proper authority.
Clear briefs agreeing with each other can still fail by jointly omitting an obligation.

Analysis can finish with bounded unknowns. An implementation issue cannot leave
a shared choice unresolved, including when marked Planned. A design/evidence
request can gather missing input but does not satisfy implementation completeness.
A short brief passes only when it preserves the context needed to implement.

## Evidence and changed inputs

Distinguish proposed, accepted, implemented, deployed and verified claims with
scoped evidence, not a new status protocol. Reuse established prerequisite proof
while its basis remains valid. A closed issue, mention, green CI or bare assertion
alone does not prove an outcome beyond what its artifacts establish.

Revalidate what changed: contract, relevant source, dependency, assignment meaning
or execution environment. Do not refresh unrelated evidence or require repeated
approval merely because a stage or session changed. New obligations cannot
retroactively redefine accepted work. A partial publication or unavailable tracker
retains the pending action instead of implying completion.

## Outcome review

At handoff, check recipient understanding, shared compatibility and coverage.
At acceptance, check the delivered behavior against those same obligations,
reconcile the affected records and reassess dependents. Independence of review
does not prove correctness by itself; retain concrete findings and counterexamples.
